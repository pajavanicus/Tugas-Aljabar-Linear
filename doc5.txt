The strategic landscape of the Miami Grand Prix was completely transformed by a major incident during the early stages of the race. On lap six, the race was neutralized following a heavy crash involving Isack Hadjar. The young driver misjudged his entry into the tight chicane section, clipping the wall on the left side, which instantly broke his front suspension.

Unable to steer his damaged car, Hadjar collided heavily with the safety barriers on the outside of Turn 15. The severity of the impact and the amount of debris scattered across the racing line left the race director with no choice but to deploy the Safety Car. This sudden interruption bunched the pack together and erased the gaps that the leaders had meticulously built during the opening laps.

The Safety Car period triggered a flurry of activity in the pit lane, as teams scrambled to adapt their strategies. Drivers who had started on the softer tyre compounds took the opportunity to make a "cheap" pit stop, while those on the harder rubber had to decide whether to sacrifice track position for fresh tyres. This single incident reshuffled the running order and set the stage for the dramatic strategic battles that defined the remainder of the Grand Prix.
