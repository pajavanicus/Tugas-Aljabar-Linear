The battle for the remaining positions in the upper half of the points classification at the Miami Grand Prix was intensely competitive, featuring mixed fortunes for Mercedes and Ferrari. George Russell, driving the second Mercedes, executed a quiet but highly effective race strategy. By managing his pace and executing precise overtakes in the final sector, Russell climbed the order late in the race to secure a solid fourth-place finish, ensuring a massive points haul for his team.

Conversely, Charles Leclerc experienced a race of dramatic highs and lows for Scuderia Ferrari. The Monegasque driver made a spectacular start from the second row of the grid, immediately challenging the leaders and showing strong pace in the early stints. For a significant portion of the race, Leclerc looked poised to secure a comfortable podium finish.

Unfortunately for Ferrari, Leclerc's race unraveled in the closing stages. While running in third position and pushing hard to maintain a gap to the cars behind, he lost control of his car and spun completely unassisted on Lap 56. This costly error dropped him down the order, forcing him to settle for eighth place by the time the checkered flag fell, marking a disappointing end to a promising weekend.
